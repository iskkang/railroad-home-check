    How to interpret this report

    Distances are approximate straight-line distances to different mapped features. Train and vehicle counts refer to their reported locations and periods. Quiet-zone rules restrict routine horns but do not eliminate train noise. TMAS profiles are historical reference station patterns, not verified counts for the property road. Visit the home at different times to assess actual noise and vibration.

    WHAT THE REPORT SHOWS

    Road traffic and rail activity near a property

    Railroad Home Check turns official Federal Railroad Administration records into a practical property report. It helps buyers identify questions worth investigating before an offer or inspection.

    Reported train frequency near an address

    Review FRA estimates for daytime and nighttime thru-train movements at nearby reported crossings. The figures are historical estimates, not a live train schedule.

    Railroad quiet-zone status

    See whether the nearest reported crossing has a federal quiet-zone designation. Quiet zones restrict routine horn sounding but do not guarantee silence.

    Crossing distance, speed, and incidents

    Compare straight-line distance to the nearest reported grade crossing, available train-speed fields, and recent six-year crossing incidents.

    COMMON QUESTIONS

    Buying a home near railroad tracks

    How can I check train activity near a house?

    Enter a complete U.S. property or listing address. The tool locates the property with the U.S. Census Geocoder and checks nearby FRA grade-crossing records for reported train movements and other available fields.

    Can I check the distance to railroad tracks?

    Yes. Load surrounding data to estimate distance to the nearest mapped rail centerline. Crossing distance is reported separately. Both use an approximate geocoded property point and are not survey measurements.

    What does a railroad quiet zone mean?

    A federal quiet-zone designation restricts routine locomotive horn sounding at covered public crossings. Horns may still sound in emergencies and other permitted circumstances, so a quiet zone is not a guarantee of silence.

    Are the reported train counts live?

    No. Train counts are FRA-reported estimates, and the report displays the train-count year when available. Rail operations can change after the reported year.

    Can this predict railroad noise or vibration inside a home?

    No. Actual conditions depend on terrain, buildings, windows, train operations, horns, switching, and other local factors. Visit the property at different times and ask nearby residents before making a decision.
