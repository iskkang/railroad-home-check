How to read this report

Railroad Home Check uses the Federal Railroad Administration's Grade Crossing Inventory. Train counts are reported estimates, not live schedules. A quiet-zone designation means routine horn sounding is restricted at covered public crossings, but horns may still be used in emergencies and other permitted circumstances. Distance is straight-line distance from the geocoded property point to the crossing—not distance to the rail line itself.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Buying a home near railroad tracks

How can I check train activity near a house?

Enter a complete U.S. property or listing address. The tool locates the property with the U.S. Census Geocoder and checks nearby FRA grade-crossing records for reported train movements and other available fields.

Does the report show the distance to railroad tracks?

No. It measures straight-line distance from the geocoded property point to nearby reported grade crossings. A rail line may be closer than the nearest crossing.

What does a railroad quiet zone mean?

A federal quiet-zone designation restricts routine locomotive horn sounding at covered public crossings. Horns may still sound in emergencies and other permitted circumstances, so a quiet zone is not a guarantee of silence.

Are the reported train counts live?

No. Train counts are FRA-reported estimates, and the report displays the train-count year when available. Rail operations can change after the reported year.

Can this predict railroad noise or vibration inside a home?

No. Actual conditions depend on terrain, buildings, windows, train operations, horns, switching, and other local factors. Visit the property at different times and ask nearby residents before making a decision.
