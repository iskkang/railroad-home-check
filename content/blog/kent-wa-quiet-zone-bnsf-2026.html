What happened

Mayor Dana Ralph told the City Council on June 16, 2026 that the Federal Railroad Administration had approved Kent's quiet zone, and it took effect the week of August 24. The zone runs from S. 212th Street in the north to S. 259th Street in the south and covers eight crossings on the BNSF mainline: S. 212th, James, Smith, Meeker, Gowe, Titus, Willis (SR 516) and S. 259th.

The city put about $3.5 million into raised medians, pavement markings and signs, roughly $2.5 million of it from state grants. Residents of the Mill Creek neighborhood first raised horn noise with the council in 2011. The Union Pacific tracks west of downtown are not part of the zone (Kent Reporter).

Before the quiet zone, a train on this line sounded its horn at each of the eight crossings it passed. With about 21 trains between 6 PM and 6 AM, homes along this stretch heard horn sequences many times a night. The 10 highway-rail incidents FRA reports at these crossings since January 2021 are the other side of the story: quiet zones require extra safety measures precisely because the horn is a warning.

Our take: the change is real, the records lag

As of the inventory revision dated October 12, 2026, about seven weeks after the zone took effect, all eight crossings are still filed as "no quiet zone." Federal data is updated by the railroads and states on their own schedule, so a buyer reading it today would underestimate how quiet downtown Kent has become at night.

For anyone looking at homes in Kent:

  1. Near the BNSF line between S. 212th and S. 259th: routine horns should be gone, but about 42 trains a day still pass, with crossing bells and the sound of the trains themselves. A night visit tells you more than any record.
  2. Near the Union Pacific tracks west of downtown: nothing changed. The normal horn rule still applies there.
  3. Anywhere near tracks: check the nearest crossing for a specific address rather than the city average. Distance to the crossing matters more than distance to the track.
