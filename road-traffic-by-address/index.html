Road Traffic Counts Near an Address

Look beyond a road’s appearance at an open house. Reported vehicle counts provide a starting point for investigating traffic around a home.

How to check nearby road traffic

Enter a complete U.S. address on the property checker, or choose the location on the map. In the Sound tab, the map loads nearby reported road segments and mapped rail lines. Select a colored road to see its count and reporting year. Open Road traffic below the map to compare the nearest returned segments.

What does AADT mean?

Annual average daily traffic (AADT) is a yearly average of daily vehicle traffic at a reported road segment. A count of 30,000 vehicles per day is not a prediction that precisely 30,000 cars will pass the property tomorrow. It does not identify rush-hour congestion, vehicle speeds, or the split between daytime and nighttime trips.

Read the road location and year together

The checker returns road data within one mile of the approximate property pin. Distance is to a mapped road centerline. Rows can describe neighboring sections of the same road; do not add their counts together. The nearest road with a reported count may not be the street immediately outside the house. An uncolored street can have traffic even when no count is available.

Map colors show volume bands

Blue is below 5,000 daily vehicles; teal is 5,000–14,999; yellow is 15,000–29,999; orange is 30,000–59,999; red is 60,000 or more. Gray overlays have no reported count. These fixed bands help compare returned segments. They are not noise limits, health thresholds or safety ratings.

Trucks and overnight activity need separate checks

Single-unit truck/bus and combination-truck counts appear when available. Missing values mean not reported. The optional hourly road section is a December 2023 reference sample from nearby TMAS stations, which may not be on the property road. It is not a current overnight count for the home.

What to verify at a viewing

Visit during commuter hours and late evening. Listen indoors and outside; note truck routes, intersections, deliveries and bedroom orientation. Road traffic counts alone cannot establish noise inside a building.

Source context: FHWA Highway Performance Monitoring System and FHWA TMAS data.
