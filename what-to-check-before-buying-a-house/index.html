What to Check Before Buying a House

Start here

Start with three separate checks: the condition of the building, the surroundings you will live with, and the documents and costs you need to verify. An address report covers only part of that work.

1. Separate what you know from what you assume

Create a short list for each candidate: confirmed, needs a document, needs a visit. A renovated kitchen says little about drainage, nearby freight activity or a roof. Record who supplied each answer and when.

Before deciding, identify the issues that would change your decision: a difficult driveway, a bedroom facing a busy street, recurring water entry, or a repair you cannot take on. Investigate those first.

2. Check the location at the correct point

Run an address check and confirm the map pin against the building and street. Look at reported road traffic, nearby rail lines, public crossings and available flood records. Read the reporting year and coverage note for each result.

A count on a nearby road segment does not describe every street. A train estimate at a crossing is not a timetable at the house. An unavailable result is an unanswered question, not evidence that there is no issue.

3. Inspect the building separately

Ask an independent inspector to assess the physical condition of the home. CFPB distinguishes a home inspection from an appraisal: they serve different purposes. Neither a surroundings report nor listing photos replace that inspection.

Prepare questions about visible moisture, roof condition, heating and cooling, drainage and any changes to the building. Ask the inspector what was inaccessible or outside the inspection scope, then decide which specialist follow-up is needed.

4. Make a second visit with a purpose

Return at a different time, with permission to access the property. Try the actual commute and driveway. Stand in the rooms you expect to use for sleep or work. Note the time, weather, windows open or closed, and the specific observation; avoid turning one quiet visit into a general conclusion.

5. Close the gaps before committing

Keep the inspection report, official records and your questions together. Discuss unresolved contract, insurance and transaction questions with the relevant professional. Use the checklist below to prepare that discussion, rather than treating a green map layer as an approval to buy.
