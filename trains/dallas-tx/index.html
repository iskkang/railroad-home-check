What this means for a home search in Dallas

Confirm the crossing, not the neighborhood. Quiet-zone status is reported crossing by crossing (5 of 217 here). Engineers can still sound horns for emergencies, and a home can sit between a quiet-zone crossing and one that is not. How quiet zones work.

The highest counts come from passenger rail. 105 of the 217 crossings are on transit or commuter lines such as DART, Trinity Railway Express, with counts up to 239 trains a day. Railroad-company lines are listed separately below.

Many counts are old. 104 of 159 active crossings carry a count year before 2021 or none at all (range 2000–2026). Ask the seller or neighbors whether traffic has changed.

Crossing incidents. FRA accident reports list 40 highway-rail incidents at 20 of these crossings between January 2021 and August 2026. That is a safety record for vehicles and pedestrians, not a noise measure. How to read crossing accident history.

Common questions about trains in Dallas

Do trains run at night in Dallas?

156 of 159 crossings with reported traffic list trains between 6 PM and 6 AM. Check the specific crossing near a home, because night traffic varies by line.

Are there railroad quiet zones in Dallas?

5 public crossings are reported in an FRA quiet zone. Status is reported per crossing, so confirm the crossing nearest the address.

Which railroads operate in Dallas?

By number of public crossings: DART (92), Dallas, Garland & Northeastern Railroad (38), Union Pacific (29), BNSF (25).

How fast do trains go through Dallas?

The highest maximum timetable speed reported at these crossings is 79 mph. Many urban crossings report much lower limits; the busiest-crossing list shows the speed for each.

Sources and limits

Crossing counts come from the FRA Crossing Inventory (Form 71, current), filtered to open, public, at-grade crossings whose inventory city is Dallas (records reported as in or near the city). Incident counts come from FRA highway-rail accident reports (Form 57), January 2021–August 2026. Train counts are railroad-reported estimates with the listed count year; they are not live schedules, noise levels or safety ratings. Read our source coverage and limitations and corrections policy.
