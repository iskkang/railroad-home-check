What this means for a home search in San Antonio

Confirm the crossing, not the neighborhood. Quiet-zone status is reported crossing by crossing (77 of 209 here). Engineers can still sound horns for emergencies, and a home can sit between a quiet-zone crossing and one that is not. How quiet zones work.

Crossing incidents. FRA accident reports list 60 highway-rail incidents at 40 of these crossings between January 2021 and August 2026. That is a safety record for vehicles and pedestrians, not a noise measure. How to read crossing accident history.

Common questions about trains in San Antonio

Do trains run at night in San Antonio?

194 of 194 crossings with reported traffic list trains between 6 PM and 6 AM. Check the specific crossing near a home, because night traffic varies by line.

Are there railroad quiet zones in San Antonio?

77 public crossings are reported in an FRA quiet zone. Status is reported per crossing, so confirm the crossing nearest the address.

Which railroads operate in San Antonio?

By number of public crossings: Union Pacific (208), U.S. Department of Defense (1).

How fast do trains go through San Antonio?

The highest maximum timetable speed reported at these crossings is 79 mph. Many urban crossings report much lower limits; the busiest-crossing list shows the speed for each.

Is San Antonio a noisy place to live?

About 3% of San Antonio residents live where modeled transportation noise averages 55 dB or more over 24 hours (USDOT 2020 noise map), about the same as the median city among the 299 we cover. Rail is the biggest single source. Noise varies block by block, so check the specific address.

How hot does it get in San Antonio?

San Antonio averages about 134 days a year at 90°F or hotter and 34 at 100°F or hotter, with summer highs near 96°F (NOAA 1991–2020 normals).

Does it freeze or snow in San Antonio?

About 14 nights a year drop below freezing. Winter nights average 44°F.

Sources and limits

Crossing counts come from the FRA Crossing Inventory (Form 71, current), filtered to open, public, at-grade crossings whose inventory city is San Antonio (records reported as in or near the city). Incident counts come from FRA highway-rail accident reports (Form 57), January 2021–August 2026. Train counts are railroad-reported estimates with the listed count year; they are not live schedules, noise levels or safety ratings. Read our source coverage and limitations and corrections policy.
