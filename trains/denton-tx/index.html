What this means for a home search in Denton

Confirm the crossing, not the neighborhood. Quiet-zone status is reported crossing by crossing (10 of 40 here). Engineers can still sound horns for emergencies, and a home can sit between a quiet-zone crossing and one that is not. How quiet zones work.

The highest counts come from passenger rail. 12 of the 40 crossings are on transit or commuter lines, with counts up to 67 trains a day. Railroad-company lines are listed separately below.

Crossing incidents. FRA accident reports list 6 highway-rail incidents at 3 of these crossings between January 2021 and August 2026. That is a safety record for vehicles and pedestrians, not a noise measure. How to read crossing accident history.

Common questions about trains in Denton

Do trains run at night in Denton?

37 of 37 crossings with reported traffic list trains between 6 PM and 6 AM. Check the specific crossing near a home, because night traffic varies by line.

Are there railroad quiet zones in Denton?

10 public crossings are reported in an FRA quiet zone. Status is reported per crossing, so confirm the crossing nearest the address.

Which railroads operate in Denton?

By number of public crossings: Union Pacific (18), Denton County Transportation Authority (12), Kansas City Southern (CPKC) (10).

How fast do trains go through Denton?

The highest maximum timetable speed reported at these crossings is 60 mph. Many urban crossings report much lower limits; the busiest-crossing list shows the speed for each.

Sources and limits

Crossing counts come from the FRA Crossing Inventory (Form 71, current), filtered to open, public, at-grade crossings whose inventory city is Denton (records reported as in or near the city). Incident counts come from FRA highway-rail accident reports (Form 57), January 2021–August 2026. Train counts are railroad-reported estimates with the listed count year; they are not live schedules, noise levels or safety ratings. Read our source coverage and limitations and corrections policy.
