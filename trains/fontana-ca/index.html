What this means for a home search in Fontana

Horns are the default. None of the 22 public crossings is reported in an FRA quiet zone, so routine horn sounding at public crossings applies under the federal Train Horn Rule. Distance to the nearest crossing matters more here than in cities with broad quiet-zone coverage. Train horns near a house.

The highest counts come from passenger rail. 8 of the 22 crossings are on transit or commuter lines such as Metrolink (SCRRA), with counts up to 43 trains a day. Railroad-company lines report little traffic here, so the busiest-crossing list includes every operator.

Crossing incidents. FRA accident reports list 1 highway-rail incident at 1 of these crossings between January 2021 and August 2026. That is a safety record for vehicles and pedestrians, not a noise measure. How to read crossing accident history.

Common questions about trains in Fontana

Do trains run at night in Fontana?

8 of 8 crossings with reported traffic list trains between 6 PM and 6 AM. Check the specific crossing near a home, because night traffic varies by line.

Are there railroad quiet zones in Fontana?

None of the 22 public crossings in this set is reported in an FRA quiet zone, so routine horn sounding is the default at public crossings.

Which railroads operate in Fontana?

By number of public crossings: Union Pacific (12), Metrolink (SCRRA) (8), BNSF (2).

How fast do trains go through Fontana?

The highest maximum timetable speed reported at these crossings is 79 mph. Many urban crossings report much lower limits; the busiest-crossing list shows the speed for each.

How hot does it get in Fontana?

Fontana averages about 93 days a year at 90°F or hotter and 19 at 100°F or hotter, with summer highs near 92°F (NOAA 1991–2020 normals).

Does it freeze or snow in Fontana?

About 2 nights a year drop below freezing. Winter nights average 45°F.
