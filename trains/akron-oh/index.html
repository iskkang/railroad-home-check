What this means for a home search in Akron

Horns are the default. None of the 35 public crossings is reported in an FRA quiet zone, so routine horn sounding at public crossings applies under the federal Train Horn Rule. Distance to the nearest crossing matters more here than in cities with broad quiet-zone coverage. Train horns near a house.

Common questions about trains in Akron

Do trains run at night in Akron?

13 of 26 crossings with reported traffic list trains between 6 PM and 6 AM. Check the specific crossing near a home, because night traffic varies by line.

Are there railroad quiet zones in Akron?

None of the 35 public crossings in this set is reported in an FRA quiet zone, so routine horn sounding is the default at public crossings.

Which railroads operate in Akron?

By number of public crossings: Wheeling & Lake Erie Railway (11), Metro Regional Transit Authority (9), Cuyahoga Valley Scenic Railway (8), Akron Barberton Cluster Railway (6).

How fast do trains go through Akron?

The highest maximum timetable speed reported at these crossings is 40 mph. Many urban crossings report much lower limits; the busiest-crossing list shows the speed for each.

How hot does it get in Akron?

Akron averages about 14 days a year at 90°F or hotter, with summer highs near 83°F (NOAA 1991–2020 normals).

Does it freeze or snow in Akron?

About 118 nights a year drop below freezing. Winter nights average 23°F.

Sources and limits

Crossing counts come from the FRA Crossing Inventory (Form 71, current), filtered to open, public, at-grade crossings whose inventory city is Akron (records reported as in or near the city). Incident counts come from FRA highway-rail accident reports (Form 57), January 2021–August 2026. Train counts are railroad-reported estimates with the listed count year; they are not live schedules, noise levels or safety ratings. Read our source coverage and limitations and corrections policy.
