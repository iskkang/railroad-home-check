What this means for a home search in Tulsa

Confirm the crossing, not the neighborhood. Quiet-zone status is reported crossing by crossing (5 of 123 here). Engineers can still sound horns for emergencies, and a home can sit between a quiet-zone crossing and one that is not. How quiet zones work.

Common questions about trains in Tulsa

Do trains run at night in Tulsa?

55 of 63 crossings with reported traffic list trains between 6 PM and 6 AM. Check the specific crossing near a home, because night traffic varies by line.

Are there railroad quiet zones in Tulsa?

5 public crossings are reported in an FRA quiet zone. Status is reported per crossing, so confirm the crossing nearest the address.

Which railroads operate in Tulsa?

By number of public crossings: BNSF (39), Union Pacific (29), Tulsa-Sapulpa Union Railway (21), Sand Springs Railway (17).

How fast do trains go through Tulsa?

The highest maximum timetable speed reported at these crossings is 60 mph. Many urban crossings report much lower limits; the busiest-crossing list shows the speed for each.

Is Tulsa a noisy place to live?

About 2% of Tulsa residents live where modeled transportation noise averages 55 dB or more over 24 hours (USDOT 2020 noise map), less than the median city among the 299 we cover. Rail is the biggest single source. Noise varies block by block, so check the specific address.

How hot does it get in Tulsa?

Tulsa averages about 74 days a year at 90°F or hotter and 11 at 100°F or hotter, with summer highs near 92°F (NOAA 1991–2020 normals).

Does it freeze or snow in Tulsa?

About 72 nights a year drop below freezing, and normal snowfall is 9 inches a year. Winter nights average 30°F.

Sources and limits

Incident counts come from FRA highway-rail accident reports (Form 57), January 2021–August 2026. Train counts are railroad-reported estimates with the listed count year; they are not live schedules, noise levels or safety ratings. Read our source coverage and limitations and corrections policy.
