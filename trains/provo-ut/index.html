What this means for a home search in Provo

Confirm the crossing, not the neighborhood. Quiet-zone status is reported crossing by crossing (9 of 16 here). Engineers can still sound horns for emergencies, and a home can sit between a quiet-zone crossing and one that is not. How quiet zones work.

The highest counts come from passenger rail. 9 of the 16 crossings are on transit or commuter lines such as UTA FrontRunner, with counts up to 81 trains a day. Railroad-company lines report little traffic here, so the busiest-crossing list includes every operator.

Many counts are old. 8 of 15 active crossings carry a count year before 2021 or none at all (range 2015–2026). Ask the seller or neighbors whether traffic has changed.

Crossing incidents. FRA accident reports list 2 highway-rail incidents at 2 of these crossings between January 2021 and August 2026. That is a safety record for vehicles and pedestrians, not a noise measure. How to read crossing accident history.

Common questions about trains in Provo

Do trains run at night in Provo?

14 of 15 crossings with reported traffic list trains between 6 PM and 6 AM. Check the specific crossing near a home, because night traffic varies by line.

Are there railroad quiet zones in Provo?

9 public crossings are reported in an FRA quiet zone. Status is reported per crossing, so confirm the crossing nearest the address.

Which railroads operate in Provo?

By number of public crossings: UTA FrontRunner (9), Union Pacific (6), Heber Valley Railroad (1).

How fast do trains go through Provo?

The highest maximum timetable speed reported at these crossings is 79 mph. Many urban crossings report much lower limits; the busiest-crossing list shows the speed for each.

How hot does it get in Provo?

Provo averages about 74 days a year at 90°F or hotter and 9 at 100°F or hotter, with summer highs near 92°F (NOAA 1991–2020 normals).

Does it freeze or snow in Provo?

About 111 nights a year drop below freezing, and normal snowfall is 45 inches a year. Winter nights average 25°F.
