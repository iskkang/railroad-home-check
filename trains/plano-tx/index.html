What this means for a home search in Plano

Confirm the crossing, not the neighborhood. Quiet-zone status is reported crossing by crossing (18 of 33 here). Engineers can still sound horns for emergencies, and a home can sit between a quiet-zone crossing and one that is not. How quiet zones work.

The highest counts come from passenger rail. 27 of the 33 crossings are on transit or commuter lines such as DART Silverline, DART, with counts up to 220 trains a day. Railroad-company lines report little traffic here, so the busiest-crossing list includes every operator.

Common questions about trains in Plano

Do trains run at night in Plano?

28 of 28 crossings with reported traffic list trains between 6 PM and 6 AM. Check the specific crossing near a home, because night traffic varies by line.

Are there railroad quiet zones in Plano?

18 public crossings are reported in an FRA quiet zone. Status is reported per crossing, so confirm the crossing nearest the address.

Which railroads operate in Plano?

By number of public crossings: DART Silverline (15), DART (12), Kansas City Southern (CPKC) (6).

How fast do trains go through Plano?

The highest maximum timetable speed reported at these crossings is 65 mph. Many urban crossings report much lower limits; the busiest-crossing list shows the speed for each.

Is Plano a noisy place to live?

About 3% of Plano residents live where modeled transportation noise averages 55 dB or more over 24 hours (USDOT 2020 noise map), about the same as the median city among the 299 we cover. Roads are the biggest single source. Noise varies block by block, so check the specific address.

How hot does it get in Plano?

Plano averages about 97 days a year at 90°F or hotter and 17 at 100°F or hotter, with summer highs near 94°F (NOAA 1991–2020 normals).

Does it freeze or snow in Plano?

About 48 nights a year drop below freezing. Winter nights average 36°F.
