Trains near a Louisville home: what the records show

The FRA inventory lists 144 public at-grade crossings in or near Louisville. 82 report trains between 6 PM and 6 AM, and 35 sit in a quiet zone. On railroad-company lines, a typical active crossing reports about 7 trains a day; the busiest reports 28.

What this means for a home search in Louisville

Confirm the crossing, not the neighborhood. Quiet-zone status is reported crossing by crossing (35 of 144 here). Engineers can still sound horns for emergencies, and a home can sit between a quiet-zone crossing and one that is not. How quiet zones work.

Crossing incidents. FRA accident reports list 19 highway-rail incidents at 15 of these crossings between January 2021 and August 2026. That is a safety record for vehicles and pedestrians, not a noise measure. How to read crossing accident history.

Common questions about trains in Louisville

Do trains run at night in Louisville?

82 of 95 crossings with reported traffic list trains between 6 PM and 6 AM. Check the specific crossing near a home, because night traffic varies by line.

Are there railroad quiet zones in Louisville?

35 public crossings are reported in an FRA quiet zone. Status is reported per crossing, so confirm the crossing nearest the address.

Which railroads operate in Louisville?

By number of public crossings: CSX (70), Norfolk Southern (39), Paducah & Louisville Railway (17), R.J. Corman (10).

How fast do trains go through Louisville?

The highest maximum timetable speed reported at these crossings is 60 mph. Many urban crossings report much lower limits; the busiest-crossing list shows the speed for each.

Is Louisville a noisy place to live?

About 17% of Louisville residents live where modeled transportation noise averages 55 dB or more over 24 hours (USDOT 2020 noise map), more than the median city among the 299 we cover. Roads are the biggest single source. Noise varies block by block, so check the specific address.

How hot does it get in Louisville?

Louisville averages about 43 days a year at 90°F or hotter and 1 at 100°F or hotter, with summer highs near 88°F (NOAA 1991–2020 normals).

Does it freeze or snow in Louisville?

About 74 nights a year drop below freezing, and normal snowfall is 13 inches a year. Winter nights average 30°F.

Sources and limits

Crossing counts come from the FRA Crossing Inventory (Form 71, current), filtered to open, public, at-grade crossings whose inventory city is Louisville (records reported as in or near the city). Incident counts come from FRA highway-rail accident reports (Form 57), January 2021–August 2026. Train counts are railroad-reported estimates with the listed count year; they are not live schedules, noise levels or safety ratings. Read our source coverage and limitations and corrections policy.
