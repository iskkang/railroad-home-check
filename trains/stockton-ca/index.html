What this means for a home search in Stockton

Confirm the crossing, not the neighborhood. Quiet-zone status is reported crossing by crossing (1 of 134 here). Engineers can still sound horns for emergencies, and a home can sit between a quiet-zone crossing and one that is not. How quiet zones work.

Many counts are old. 47 of 102 active crossings carry a count year before 2021 or none at all (range 2002–2025). Ask the seller or neighbors whether traffic has changed.

Crossing incidents. FRA accident reports list 35 highway-rail incidents at 24 of these crossings between January 2021 and August 2026. That is a safety record for vehicles and pedestrians, not a noise measure. How to read crossing accident history.

Common questions about trains in Stockton

Do trains run at night in Stockton?

84 of 102 crossings with reported traffic list trains between 6 PM and 6 AM. Check the specific crossing near a home, because night traffic varies by line.

Are there railroad quiet zones in Stockton?

1 public crossings are reported in an FRA quiet zone. Status is reported per crossing, so confirm the crossing nearest the address.

Which railroads operate in Stockton?

By number of public crossings: Central California Traction (49), Union Pacific (34), Stockton Terminal & Eastern Railroad (28), BNSF (19).

How fast do trains go through Stockton?

The highest maximum timetable speed reported at these crossings is 79 mph. Many urban crossings report much lower limits; the busiest-crossing list shows the speed for each.

How hot does it get in Stockton?

Stockton averages about 88 days a year at 90°F or hotter and 19 at 100°F or hotter, with summer highs near 93°F (NOAA 1991–2020 normals).

Does it freeze or snow in Stockton?

About 19 nights a year drop below freezing. Winter nights average 40°F.

Sources and limits
