Trains near a Austin home: what the records show

The FRA inventory lists 83 public at-grade crossings in or near Austin. 64 report trains between 6 PM and 6 AM, and 59 sit in a quiet zone. Most activity here is light rail and commuter service; the busiest crossing reports 52 trains a day.

What this means for a home search in Austin

Confirm the crossing, not the neighborhood. Quiet-zone status is reported crossing by crossing (59 of 83 here). Engineers can still sound horns for emergencies, and a home can sit between a quiet-zone crossing and one that is not. How quiet zones work.

The highest counts come from passenger rail. 62 of the 83 crossings are on transit or commuter lines such as CapMetro, with counts up to 52 trains a day. Railroad-company lines report little traffic here, so the busiest-crossing list includes every operator.

Crossing incidents. FRA accident reports list 14 highway-rail incidents at 10 of these crossings between January 2021 and August 2026. That is a safety record for vehicles and pedestrians, not a noise measure. How to read crossing accident history.

Common questions about trains in Austin

Do trains run at night in Austin?

64 of 64 crossings with reported traffic list trains between 6 PM and 6 AM. Check the specific crossing near a home, because night traffic varies by line.

Are there railroad quiet zones in Austin?

59 public crossings are reported in an FRA quiet zone. Status is reported per crossing, so confirm the crossing nearest the address.

Which railroads operate in Austin?

By number of public crossings: CapMetro (62), Union Pacific (21).

How fast do trains go through Austin?

The highest maximum timetable speed reported at these crossings is 70 mph. Many urban crossings report much lower limits; the busiest-crossing list shows the speed for each.

Sources and limits

Crossing counts come from the FRA Crossing Inventory (Form 71, current), filtered to open, public, at-grade crossings whose inventory city is Austin (records reported as in or near the city). Incident counts come from FRA highway-rail accident reports (Form 57), January 2021–August 2026. Train counts are railroad-reported estimates with the listed count year; they are not live schedules, noise levels or safety ratings. Read our source coverage and limitations and corrections policy.
