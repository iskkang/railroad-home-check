What this means for a home search in Gary

Horns are the default. None of the 89 public crossings is reported in an FRA quiet zone, so routine horn sounding at public crossings applies under the federal Train Horn Rule. Distance to the nearest crossing matters more here than in cities with broad quiet-zone coverage. Train horns near a house.

The highest counts come from passenger rail. 17 of the 89 crossings are on transit or commuter lines such as South Shore Line (NICTD), with counts up to 74 trains a day. Railroad-company lines are listed separately below.

Many counts are old. 37 of 74 active crossings carry a count year before 2021 or none at all (range 2015–2026). Ask the seller or neighbors whether traffic has changed.

Crossing incidents. FRA accident reports list 55 highway-rail incidents at 24 of these crossings between January 2021 and August 2026. That is a safety record for vehicles and pedestrians, not a noise measure. How to read crossing accident history.

Common questions about trains in Gary

Do trains run at night in Gary?

66 of 74 crossings with reported traffic list trains between 6 PM and 6 AM. Check the specific crossing near a home, because night traffic varies by line.

Are there railroad quiet zones in Gary?

None of the 89 public crossings in this set is reported in an FRA quiet zone, so routine horn sounding is the default at public crossings.

Which railroads operate in Gary?

By number of public crossings: Norfolk Southern (21), CSX (19), South Shore Line (NICTD) (17), Chicago, Ft. Wayne & Eastern (14).

How fast do trains go through Gary?

The highest maximum timetable speed reported at these crossings is 79 mph. Many urban crossings report much lower limits; the busiest-crossing list shows the speed for each.

Is Gary a noisy place to live?

About 5% of Gary residents live where modeled transportation noise averages 55 dB or more over 24 hours (USDOT 2020 noise map), more than the median city among the 299 we cover. Rail is the biggest single source. Noise varies block by block, so check the specific address.

Sources and limits

Crossing counts come from the FRA Crossing Inventory (Form 71, current), filtered to open, public, at-grade crossings whose inventory city is Gary (records reported as in or near the city). Incident counts come from FRA highway-rail accident reports (Form 57), January 2021–August 2026.
