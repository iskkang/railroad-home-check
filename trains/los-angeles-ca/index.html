What this means for a home search in Los Angeles

Horns are the default. None of the 171 public crossings is reported in an FRA quiet zone, so routine horn sounding at public crossings applies under the federal Train Horn Rule. Distance to the nearest crossing matters more here than in cities with broad quiet-zone coverage. Train horns near a house.

The highest counts come from passenger rail. 49 of the 171 crossings are on transit or commuter lines such as Metrolink (SCRRA), LA Metro, with counts up to 208 trains a day. Railroad-company lines are listed separately below.

Many counts are old. 43 of 90 active crossings carry a count year before 2021 or none at all (range 2002–2025). Ask the seller or neighbors whether traffic has changed.

Crossing incidents. FRA accident reports list 40 highway-rail incidents at 30 of these crossings between January 2021 and August 2026. That is a safety record for vehicles and pedestrians, not a noise measure. How to read crossing accident history.

Common questions about trains in Los Angeles

Do trains run at night in Los Angeles?

80 of 90 crossings with reported traffic list trains between 6 PM and 6 AM. Check the specific crossing near a home, because night traffic varies by line.

Are there railroad quiet zones in Los Angeles?

None of the 171 public crossings in this set is reported in an FRA quiet zone, so routine horn sounding is the default at public crossings.

Which railroads operate in Los Angeles?

By number of public crossings: Union Pacific (70), Pacific Harbor Line (39), Metrolink (SCRRA) (36), LA Metro (13).

How fast do trains go through Los Angeles?

The highest maximum timetable speed reported at these crossings is 79 mph. Many urban crossings report much lower limits; the busiest-crossing list shows the speed for each.

How hot does it get in Los Angeles?

Los Angeles averages about 26 days a year at 90°F or hotter and 2 at 100°F or hotter, with summer highs near 81°F (NOAA 1991–2020 normals).

Does it freeze or snow in Los Angeles?

About 0 nights a year drop below freezing. Winter nights average 49°F.

Sources and limits

Crossing counts come from the FRA Crossing Inventory (Form 71, current), filtered to open, public, at-grade crossings whose inventory city is Los Angeles (records reported as in or near the city). Incident counts come from FRA highway-rail accident reports (Form 57), January 2021–August 2026. Train counts are railroad-reported estimates with the listed count year; they are not live schedules, noise levels or safety ratings. Read our source coverage and limitations and corrections policy.
