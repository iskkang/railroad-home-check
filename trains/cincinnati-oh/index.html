What this means for a home search in Cincinnati

Horns are the default. None of the 34 public crossings is reported in an FRA quiet zone, so routine horn sounding at public crossings applies under the federal Train Horn Rule. Distance to the nearest crossing matters more here than in cities with broad quiet-zone coverage. Train horns near a house.

Many counts are old. 12 of 26 active crossings carry a count year before 2021 or none at all (range 2017–2026). Ask the seller or neighbors whether traffic has changed.

Crossing incidents. FRA accident reports list 5 highway-rail incidents at 5 of these crossings between January 2021 and August 2026. That is a safety record for vehicles and pedestrians, not a noise measure. How to read crossing accident history.

Common questions about trains in Cincinnati

Do trains run at night in Cincinnati?

19 of 26 crossings with reported traffic list trains between 6 PM and 6 AM. Check the specific crossing near a home, because night traffic varies by line.

Are there railroad quiet zones in Cincinnati?

None of the 34 public crossings in this set is reported in an FRA quiet zone, so routine horn sounding is the default at public crossings.

Which railroads operate in Cincinnati?

By number of public crossings: Indiana & Ohio Railway (18), CSX (15), Norfolk Southern (1).

How fast do trains go through Cincinnati?

The highest maximum timetable speed reported at these crossings is 40 mph. Many urban crossings report much lower limits; the busiest-crossing list shows the speed for each.

Sources and limits

Crossing counts come from the FRA Crossing Inventory (Form 71, current), filtered to open, public, at-grade crossings whose inventory city is Cincinnati (records reported as in or near the city). Incident counts come from FRA highway-rail accident reports (Form 57), January 2021–August 2026. Train counts are railroad-reported estimates with the listed count year; they are not live schedules, noise levels or safety ratings. Read our source coverage and limitations and corrections policy.
