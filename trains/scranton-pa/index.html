What this means for a home search in Scranton

Horns are the default. None of the 29 public crossings is reported in an FRA quiet zone, so routine horn sounding at public crossings applies under the federal Train Horn Rule. Distance to the nearest crossing matters more here than in cities with broad quiet-zone coverage. Train horns near a house.

Many counts are old. 22 of 22 active crossings carry a count year before 2021 or none at all (range 2018–2020). Ask the seller or neighbors whether traffic has changed.

Common questions about trains in Scranton

Do trains run at night in Scranton?

19 of 22 crossings with reported traffic list trains between 6 PM and 6 AM. Check the specific crossing near a home, because night traffic varies by line.

Are there railroad quiet zones in Scranton?

None of the 29 public crossings in this set is reported in an FRA quiet zone, so routine horn sounding is the default at public crossings.

Which railroads operate in Scranton?

By number of public crossings: Delaware Lackawanna Railroad (22), Reading Blue Mountain & Northern Railroad Commission (4), Luzerne & Susquehanna Railway (3).

How fast do trains go through Scranton?

The highest maximum timetable speed reported at these crossings is 25 mph. Many urban crossings report much lower limits; the busiest-crossing list shows the speed for each.

How hot does it get in Scranton?

Scranton averages about 12 days a year at 90°F or hotter, with summer highs near 82°F (NOAA 1991–2020 normals).

Does it freeze or snow in Scranton?

About 122 nights a year drop below freezing, and normal snowfall is 45 inches a year. Winter nights average 23°F.
