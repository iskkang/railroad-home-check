Trains near a Fort Worth home: what the records show

The FRA inventory lists 177 public at-grade crossings in or near Fort Worth. 144 report trains between 6 PM and 6 AM, and 47 sit in a quiet zone. On railroad-company lines, a typical active crossing reports about 10 trains a day; the busiest reports 28.

What this means for a home search in Fort Worth

Confirm the crossing, not the neighborhood. Quiet-zone status is reported crossing by crossing (47 of 177 here). Engineers can still sound horns for emergencies, and a home can sit between a quiet-zone crossing and one that is not. How quiet zones work.

The highest counts come from passenger rail. 34 of the 177 crossings are on transit or commuter lines such as Trinity Railway Express, TEXRail, with counts up to 80 trains a day. Railroad-company lines are listed separately below.

Crossing incidents. FRA accident reports list 41 highway-rail incidents at 31 of these crossings between January 2021 and August 2026. That is a safety record for vehicles and pedestrians, not a noise measure. How to read crossing accident history.

Common questions about trains in Fort Worth

Do trains run at night in Fort Worth?

144 of 149 crossings with reported traffic list trains between 6 PM and 6 AM. Check the specific crossing near a home, because night traffic varies by line.

Are there railroad quiet zones in Fort Worth?

47 public crossings are reported in an FRA quiet zone. Status is reported per crossing, so confirm the crossing nearest the address.

Which railroads operate in Fort Worth?

By number of public crossings: Fort Worth & Western Railroad (58), Union Pacific (54), BNSF (30), Trinity Railway Express (19).

How fast do trains go through Fort Worth?

The highest maximum timetable speed reported at these crossings is 79 mph. Many urban crossings report much lower limits; the busiest-crossing list shows the speed for each.

Sources and limits

Crossing counts come from the FRA Crossing Inventory (Form 71, current), filtered to open, public, at-grade crossings whose inventory city is Fort Worth (records reported as in or near the city). Incident counts come from FRA highway-rail accident reports (Form 57), January 2021–August 2026. Train counts are railroad-reported estimates with the listed count year; they are not live schedules, noise levels or safety ratings. Read our source coverage and limitations and corrections policy.
