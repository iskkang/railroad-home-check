What this means for a home search in Peoria

Horns are the default. None of the 45 public crossings is reported in an FRA quiet zone, so routine horn sounding at public crossings applies under the federal Train Horn Rule. Distance to the nearest crossing matters more here than in cities with broad quiet-zone coverage. Train horns near a house.

Many counts are old. 26 of 36 active crossings carry a count year before 2021 or none at all (range 2017–2025). Ask the seller or neighbors whether traffic has changed.

Common questions about trains in Peoria

Do trains run at night in Peoria?

34 of 36 crossings with reported traffic list trains between 6 PM and 6 AM. Check the specific crossing near a home, because night traffic varies by line.

Are there railroad quiet zones in Peoria?

None of the 45 public crossings in this set is reported in an FRA quiet zone, so routine horn sounding is the default at public crossings.

Which railroads operate in Peoria?

By number of public crossings: Tazewell & Peoria Railroad (28), Union Pacific (7), Pioneer Industrial Railway (4), Central Illinois Railroad (3).

How fast do trains go through Peoria?

The highest maximum timetable speed reported at these crossings is 40 mph. Many urban crossings report much lower limits; the busiest-crossing list shows the speed for each.

Is Peoria a noisy place to live?

About 2% of Peoria residents live where modeled transportation noise averages 55 dB or more over 24 hours (USDOT 2020 noise map), less than the median city among the 299 we cover. Noise varies block by block, so check the specific address.

How hot does it get in Peoria?

Peoria averages about 23 days a year at 90°F or hotter and 0 at 100°F or hotter, with summer highs near 85°F (NOAA 1991–2020 normals).

Does it freeze or snow in Peoria?

About 118 nights a year drop below freezing, and normal snowfall is 26 inches a year. Winter nights average 21°F.
