How Many Trains Run Through Kansas City, MO?

The FRA crossing inventory lists 89 open public at-grade crossings in or near Kansas City. 53 report at least one through train per day and 52 report trains between 6 PM and 6 AM. On railroad-company lines (excluding transit agencies), a typical active crossing reports about 5 trains per day; the busiest reports 100.

What this means for a home search in Kansas City

Night trains are the norm, not the exception. 98% of Kansas City crossings with reported traffic also report nighttime movements. If the home you are considering sits near any active line, plan at least one visit after dark.

Quiet zones are patchy. Only 3 of 89 public crossings (3%) are reported in an FRA quiet zone. Two homes a few blocks apart can have very different horn exposure, so check the crossing closest to each address. How quiet zones work.

Most counts are recent. 51 of 53 active crossings report a train-count year of 2021 or later (range 2022–2026). Counts are still reported estimates, not schedules.

Crossing incidents. FRA highway-rail accident reports list 11 incidents at 8 of these crossings between January 2021 and August 2026. That is a safety record for vehicles and pedestrians at the crossing, not a noise measure. How to read crossing accident history.

How to check a home near the tracks in Kansas City

  1. Look up the address and find the nearest public crossing, not just the nearest track.
  2. Note the day and night counts, quiet-zone status and count year for that crossing ID.
  3. Visit once in daytime and once after 10 PM; stand outside and inside with windows closed.
  4. Ask the seller or neighbors whether traffic has changed since the count year.

Common questions about trains in Kansas City

Do trains run at night in Kansas City?

Yes on most active lines: 52 of 53 crossings with reported traffic list trains between 6 PM and 6 AM. Check the specific crossing near a home, because night traffic varies by line.

Are there railroad quiet zones in Kansas City?

3 public crossings are reported in an FRA quiet zone. Status is reported per crossing, so confirm the crossing nearest the address.

Which railroads operate in Kansas City?

By number of public crossings: Kansas City Southern (CPKC) (35), Union Pacific (31), Kansas City Terminal Railway (12), Soo Line / KCS joint agency (5).

How fast do trains go through Kansas City?

The highest maximum timetable speed reported at these crossings is 70 mph. Many urban crossings report much lower limits; the table above lists the speed for each busy crossing.

Sources and limits

Crossing counts come from the FRA Crossing Inventory (Form 71, current), filtered to open, public, at-grade crossings whose inventory city is Kansas City (records reported as in or near the city). Incident counts come from FRA highway-rail accident reports (Form 57), January 2021–August 2026. Train counts are railroad-reported estimates with the listed count year; they are not live schedules, noise levels or safety ratings. Some records omit a count year or use placeholder values.
