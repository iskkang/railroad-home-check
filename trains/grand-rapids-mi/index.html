What this means for a home search in Grand Rapids

Horns are the default. None of the 71 public crossings is reported in an FRA quiet zone, so routine horn sounding at public crossings applies under the federal Train Horn Rule. Distance to the nearest crossing matters more here than in cities with broad quiet-zone coverage. Train horns near a house.

The highest counts come from passenger rail. 19 of the 71 crossings are on transit or commuter lines such as Grand Rapids Eastern Railroad, with counts up to 7 trains a day. Railroad-company lines are listed separately below.

Crossing incidents. FRA accident reports list 3 highway-rail incidents at 3 of these crossings between January 2021 and August 2026. That is a safety record for vehicles and pedestrians, not a noise measure. How to read crossing accident history.

Common questions about trains in Grand Rapids

Do trains run at night in Grand Rapids?

43 of 50 crossings with reported traffic list trains between 6 PM and 6 AM. Check the specific crossing near a home, because night traffic varies by line.

Are there railroad quiet zones in Grand Rapids?

None of the 71 public crossings in this set is reported in an FRA quiet zone, so routine horn sounding is the default at public crossings.

Which railroads operate in Grand Rapids?

By number of public crossings: CSX (38), Grand Rapids Eastern Railroad (19), Grand Elk Railroad (12), Marquette Rail (1).

How fast do trains go through Grand Rapids?

The highest maximum timetable speed reported at these crossings is 25 mph. Many urban crossings report much lower limits; the busiest-crossing list shows the speed for each.

Sources and limits

Crossing counts come from the FRA Crossing Inventory (Form 71, current), filtered to open, public, at-grade crossings whose inventory city is Grand Rapids (records reported as in or near the city). Incident counts come from FRA highway-rail accident reports (Form 57), January 2021–August 2026.
