Trains near a Denver home: what the records show

The FRA inventory lists 174 public at-grade crossings in or near Denver. 65 report trains between 6 PM and 6 AM, and 15 sit in a quiet zone. On railroad-company lines, a typical active crossing reports about 8 trains a day; the busiest reports 28.

What this means for a home search in Denver

Confirm the crossing, not the neighborhood. Quiet-zone status is reported crossing by crossing (15 of 174 here). Engineers can still sound horns for emergencies, and a home can sit between a quiet-zone crossing and one that is not. How quiet zones work.

The highest counts come from passenger rail. 37 of the 174 crossings are on transit or commuter lines such as RTD Denver (commuter rail), RTD Denver (light rail), RTD Denver Union Station, with counts up to 428 trains a day. Railroad-company lines are listed separately below.

Crossing incidents. FRA accident reports list 35 highway-rail incidents at 23 of these crossings between January 2021 and August 2026. That is a safety record for vehicles and pedestrians, not a noise measure. How to read crossing accident history.

Common questions about trains in Denver

Do trains run at night in Denver?

65 of 67 crossings with reported traffic list trains between 6 PM and 6 AM. Check the specific crossing near a home, because night traffic varies by line.

Are there railroad quiet zones in Denver?

15 public crossings are reported in an FRA quiet zone. Status is reported per crossing, so confirm the crossing nearest the address.

Which railroads operate in Denver?

By number of public crossings: Union Pacific (69), BNSF (42), Denver Rock Island Railroad (26), RTD Denver (commuter rail) (19).

How fast do trains go through Denver?

The highest maximum timetable speed reported at these crossings is 79 mph. Many urban crossings report much lower limits; the busiest-crossing list shows the speed for each.

Sources and limits

Crossing counts come from the FRA Crossing Inventory (Form 71, current), filtered to open, public, at-grade crossings whose inventory city is Denver (records reported as in or near the city). Incident counts come from FRA highway-rail accident reports (Form 57), January 2021–August 2026. Train counts are railroad-reported estimates with the listed count year; they are not live schedules, noise levels or safety ratings. Read our source coverage and limitations and corrections policy.
