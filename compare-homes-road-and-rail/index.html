Compare Homes for Road Traffic and Rail Activity

Keep the same questions for each home. Distances, counts and reporting years answer different questions and should stay visible in your shortlist.

Photo: Aerial view of Houston, Texas, Carol M. Highsmith, Library of Congress (public domain)

Build a shortlist of up to three addresses

Run an address check and use Compare on the report. Then choose Check another address and add the next home. Comparisons stay in the current browser tab; refreshing or closing the tab clears them. The table compares available facts at each home’s nearest reported crossing. Use each individual map for its road traffic and rail-line detail.

Record the road and the crossing separately

For every property, note the nearest road with a reported count, the count year and where that road lies relative to bedrooms. Separately record distance to the nearest mapped rail line, distance to the nearest grade crossing, day/night trains and the train-count year. None of those features is interchangeable.

A worked example: which home needs another visit?

Illustrative values only, not actual listings: Home A is 0.2 miles from a mapped rail line, with 3 reported nighttime trains at its nearest crossing. Home B is 0.7 miles from a rail line, with 20 nighttime trains at its nearest crossing. The numbers alone do not show which bedroom is quieter. The crossings may be on different routes, horns may differ and building construction can change indoor sound. The useful result is a focused visit plan for each home.

Use dates to identify uncertainty

A newer count is not necessarily higher quality in every respect, but an old inventory year is a reason to ask about operational changes. If one home has a missing road count, record it as unknown. Do not replace it with zero. Counts from neighboring crossings can describe the same trains and must not be summed as if they were independent traffic.

Visit each home under comparable conditions

  • Include a weekday commuter period and a late-evening visit where feasible.
  • Listen in the same kinds of rooms, with windows open and closed.
  • Ask immediate neighbors about horns, idling, deliveries and weekend activity.
  • Write down what you observed separately from what the database reported.

Save a report with its context

Print / PDF can preserve the report shown in the browser. Share address creates a link containing the address label and coordinates; it lets the recipient run a fresh check rather than preserving a historical snapshot. Treat that link as location information and share it only where intended.

What the comparison cannot decide

The checker does not provide an appraisal, safety certification or indoor noise prediction. A quiet-zone label and a low reported count are facts to investigate, not guarantees. Read the road-count guide and the quiet-zone guide when the shortlist raises questions.
