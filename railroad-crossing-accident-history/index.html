How to Check Railroad Crossing Accident History Near a Home

Federal crossing incident records can add context to a property decision, but they should not be treated as a safety score.

Illustrative AI-generated landscape · not a photo of the searched property

Crossing IDs connect inventory and incident records

FRA grade crossings have identifying numbers that can be matched to reported highway-rail incident records. That makes it possible to see whether a nearby crossing has recent reported incidents.

One incident does not define a neighborhood

An incident record can be relevant, but frequency, road design, traffic volume and changes to warning equipment all matter. A historical count should be read as context, not as a prediction that another incident will occur.

Look at the crossing you will actually use

If a rail crossing lies on the route to school, work or emergency services, its operational impact may matter even if the house itself is not extremely close to the tracks. Long trains can also block a roadway without any accident occurring.

Combine history with current inventory data

Review crossing distance, reported train movements, speeds, quiet-zone status and recent incident history together. Then inspect the crossing in person if the property is a serious candidate.

Important: Reported incident history is not a safety certification or future-risk prediction.

Check nearby crossing records

Enter a U.S. address to review nearby FRA crossing information and recent incident counts when available.

Sources and reporting limits

Consult the FRA crossing inventory resources and FRA horn and quiet-zone guidance. Read our source coverage and limitations alongside the report.
